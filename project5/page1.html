Practice and Process

A look into my creative process

When making art, my biggest question surrounds the concept of memory. How do our experiences in relationships and society influence how we understand others and ourselves?

Sketchbook / mind map placeholder

Thought process behind my artwork

I first begin with an emotion. Whether it is negative or positive, I ground myself in the strongest emotions I feel during my creative process. It ranges from personal memory to experiences I hear and see of others.

Family traditions, childhood experiences, social media, and changing relationships in contemporary romance are recurring subjects in my work. As my projects often begin with emotions connected to personal experiences, I connect them to emotions others may recognize, such as nostalgia, discomfort, insecurity, or loss.

Development of my ideas

After selecting a specific emotion, I work through a mind map that connects my thoughts to other emotions. I use my mind map to encourage research and material experimentation before beginning creation.

I consider what I want my viewers to notice first. Would they have understood my intentions even if they did not know my background? I consider how the color palette, composition, and material could support the work’s meaning.

My ideas are never constant when it comes to mind-mapping. I rearrange composition, exaggerate certain parts, or simplify an idea for its central message to become clearer. I view these changes as an important part of the process rather than mistakes that I must avoid.
